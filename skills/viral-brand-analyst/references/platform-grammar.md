# Platform grammar

Norms, benchmarks and conventions per platform. Read the section for the platform in question — judging a LinkedIn post by TikTok norms produces nonsense, and it is the most common way a teardown goes wrong before it starts.

**On the numbers below:** they are orientation, not truth. Benchmarks move, vary enormously by niche and follower tier, and are not the same in PT-BR markets as in US ones. Use them to decide whether something is roughly normal or clearly unusual, then say what you actually observed. Never quote a benchmark from this file as if it were a measurement of the account in front of you — the account's own median always outranks any published benchmark, and computing that median is Step T2 for exactly this reason.

## 1. Instagram Reels

**What it optimizes for.** Watch time and rewatches first, then sends/shares. Saves and shares are worth far more than likes. A reel that people send to one other person outperforms a reel a lot of people like.

**Length.** 7–15s for maximum loop-ability; 30–60s when there is real substance. Both work. The failure is the middle-length reel with thin content — long enough to demand commitment, too thin to reward it.

**Retention shape.** The drop from 0 to 3 seconds is the whole ballgame; a large share of viewers leave there. A good reel holds a substantial majority through 3s. After that the curve flattens and small structural choices decide the rest.

**Engagement orientation.** Reach commonly runs several multiples of follower count for a working reel, and can run far beyond that when a post breaks out — Reels distribute heavily to non-followers, more so than most surfaces. Comment-to-view ratios are low in absolute terms, so a reel with visibly busy comments is unusual and worth investigating.

**Native conventions.** Vertical 1080×1920. Caption sits low, so keep the bottom ~15% clear of anything important. Burned-in captions are effectively mandatory — silent viewing is the default, not the exception. Cover frame matters for the grid but almost nothing for feed distribution.

**The tell for a good one.** Rewatches. If the loop is seamless, watch time can exceed the video's length, which is the strongest signal the surface has.

## 2. Instagram carousel and feed

**What it optimizes for.** Time-on-post and saves. Carousels get a second distribution chance — Instagram can re-serve them to people who did not engage the first time, which gives them a longer tail than reels.

**The metric that matters is saves.** A carousel that gets saved is reference material and will keep being served. Likes on a carousel mean much less.

**Slide count.** 8–10 is the working range. The 1→2 drop is the biggest in the sequence; nothing else comes close. Design slide 1 to be visibly incomplete.

**Format.** 4:5 (1080×1350) takes more feed height than square and is the default for a reason.

**Text density.** People read carousels differently from captions — more like a deck than a post. Big type, one idea per slide, generous negative space. Dense slides get swiped past.

## 3. TikTok

**What it optimizes for.** Completion rate above all, then rewatches, then comments. The most content-meritocratic of the major surfaces — follower count matters least here, which makes it the best place to study whether a format works on its own merits.

**Length.** Sub-15s maximizes completion. 30s+ works when the content sustains it. TikTok pushes longer content more than it used to, but completion still rules.

**Retention.** Sharper than Instagram in the first second and more forgiving after. The first frame does more work here than anywhere else.

**Sounds.** More consequential than on any other platform. Using a rising sound early is a genuine distribution advantage — and one that expires, which is exactly why it usually lands in "condicional" rather than "replicável" in the verdict.

**Comment culture.** Loudest of any platform, and the comment section is often where the real content is. Creators deliberately leave gaps for comments to fill. Check for this — a deliberate error or an obvious omission that farms corrections is a real, if cynical, technique.

**Native conventions.** Vertical. Right-side UI eats the right edge; the bottom third is caption and handle. Native-looking text beats designed text — over-produced content underperforms here in a way it does not on Instagram.

## 4. YouTube Shorts

**What it optimizes for.** Viewed-vs-swiped ratio and watch time. More forgiving of longer content than TikTok; up to 3 minutes now.

**Where it differs.** The audience arrives with more patience and more intent to actually learn something. Explainer content does better here than on TikTok. Loops matter less. Titles matter more — Shorts get searched and recommended in a way TikToks largely do not, so a Short has a much longer tail.

**Worth knowing for the verdict.** Shorts keep earning views for months. If the user's goal is durable reach rather than a spike, this is the surface, and that is a strategic recommendation the teardown can make.

## 5. YouTube long-form

**What it optimizes for.** Watch time and session time, mediated almost entirely by click-through rate on the thumbnail and title.

**The analysis is different in kind.** For long-form, thumbnail and title are not packaging — they are the majority of the outcome. A teardown that analyzes the video and skips the thumbnail has analyzed the minority of the product. Treat the thumbnail as a hook and run the hook autopsy on it.

**Retention structure.** The first 30 seconds decide most of it. Then look for the re-hook cadence — good long-form re-opens a question every few minutes. Chapters, pattern interrupts, and B-roll density all serve this.

**Benchmarks.** CTR and average view duration are the real numbers, and they are invisible unless it is the user's own channel. Say so rather than inferring them.

## 6. LinkedIn

**What it optimizes for.** Dwell time and comments, heavily weighted toward comments from people in the poster's network. Early engagement in the first hour matters more here than on any other platform.

**The "ver mais" cutoff is the entire hook problem.** Roughly the first 2–3 lines show before the fold. That is the unit to analyze — everything after it only exists for people who already clicked.

**Format conventions.** Short lines, frequent breaks, one idea per line. Reads more like poetry-shaped prose than paragraphs. External links suppress reach, so links commonly go in the first comment — check for this, it is a tell that the poster knows the platform.

**What travels.** Personal narrative with a professional lesson, contrarian takes on industry consensus, and specific numbers. Polished corporate content underperforms badly.

**Metrics are mostly invisible.** Views are shown, but reactions and comments are the usable signal. Say so rather than estimating.

**A caution.** LinkedIn virality often has no commercial value — a post can get 500k views from people who will never buy anything. The positioning question in the teardown framework matters more here than anywhere else.

## 7. X / Twitter

**What it optimizes for.** Replies and reposts, with a strong recency decay. Bookmarks are now a meaningful signal.

**The unit is the first tweet.** In a thread, tweet 1 carries essentially all the distribution; the rest is for people already committed.

**What travels.** Concision, a stated position, a surprising specific, or a screenshot. Threads have declined relative to single strong posts.

**Analysis notes.** Quote-tweets are where the real reaction lives and they are frequently the actual distribution mechanism — always check them separately from replies. A post going viral through hostile quote-tweets is a specific phenomenon with specific consequences, and it should be named as such rather than counted as success.

## 8. Cross-platform notes

**Formats do not transfer cleanly.** A TikTok reposted to Reels usually underperforms — different retention shape, different production expectations, visible watermark penalties. When a teardown recommends porting a format, say what has to change, not just that it should be ported.

**Follower-to-reach ratios differ by an order of magnitude** across these surfaces. Never compare raw view counts between platforms. Compare each post to its own account's median on its own platform.

**PT-BR market notes.** Brazil is one of the highest-engagement markets on Instagram globally, with heavier Reels consumption and notably more active comment sections than US benchmarks assume. Absolute engagement numbers from US-based benchmark articles will read as low. Treat published international benchmarks as a floor, and lean even harder on the account's own median.
