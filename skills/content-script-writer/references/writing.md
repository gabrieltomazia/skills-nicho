# A escrita

Craft notes. The structure gets someone to press record; this is what decides whether the result is watchable.

## Escrever pra ser falado

Speech and prose are different languages. A script is speech that happens to be written down, and the most common defect is prose wearing a script's formatting.

**Frases curtas.** One idea per sentence. Where prose subordinates ("Embora o deploy tenha funcionado, o cache não invalidou"), speech coordinates: "O deploy funcionou. Mas o cache não invalidou."

**Corte os conectivos de texto.** "Além disso", "portanto", "dessa forma", "vale ressaltar" — nobody says these out loud. Speech connects with "e", "mas", "aí", "só que", or with nothing at all.

**Contrações e fala real.** "Tá" instead of "está", "pra" instead of "para", "cê" where the register allows. Write what the mouth does, and stay consistent — mixing formal and casual registers in one script sounds like two people wrote it.

**Repetição é permitida.** In writing, repeating a word looks careless. In speech it lands: "Não é lento. É lento de propósito."

**O teste do fôlego.** Read each line out loud in your head. If it needs a breath the punctuation does not provide, break it. Mark the intentional pauses — they are direction, and the user recording it will thank you.

## Ritmo

Rhythm holds attention more than content does, and it is almost entirely a function of sentence length variation.

Uniform sentence length is hypnotic in the bad way. Vary deliberately: three short, then one long. The long one lands harder because of the short ones before it.

**Silence is a tool.** A beat of nothing before a payoff does more than any adjective. Mark it: `[pausa]`. Most scripts are afraid of it and are worse for it.

**Speed follows content.** Fast through the setup, slow at the turn. When rhythm and meaning break at the same moment, the viewer feels the turn instead of just hearing it — and that pairing is the most reliably copyable technique in short-form.

## Retenção

Attention decays continuously; the script's job is to keep giving reasons to stay.

**Loops abertos.** A question raised and not yet answered pulls the viewer forward. Open one early, close it before the end. Multiple small loops beat one large one — the large one asks for too much patience up front.

**Re-ganchos.** Anything over 20 seconds needs a moment that restates the stakes or opens something new. Around 0:20 and again near 0:45. Without them the middle sags, which is where most people leave.

**Densidade.** Every line earns its seconds. Cut throat-clearing ("Então, hoje eu vou falar sobre..."), cut restating what was just shown, cut the recap. If a beat can go without the video losing meaning, it goes.

**O payoff.** Deliver what the hook promised, in the video. A payoff that lives in the comments or the bio makes the video feel like bait, and the audience learns to distrust the account — expensive over time, invisible in the metrics of any single post.

**O loop final.** Ending on something that connects back to the first frame drives rewatches, which is the strongest signal short-form surfaces have. Cheap to do, and worth doing whenever the content allows it.

## Texto na tela

Most of the feed is watched on mute. On-screen text is not decoration.

**Não duplique a locução.** Text that repeats the voiceover word for word wastes a channel. Use it to add: the number while the voice gives context, the term while the voice explains it, the punchline while the voice sets it up.

**Curto.** Under ~8 words per card. People read it in a glance or not at all.

**O vídeo sobrevive no mudo?** Run this check on every script. If the meaning collapses without audio, add text until it does not.

**Marque o timing.** Text entering with the line it belongs to, not floating over three beats.

## O problema do CTA

Most CTAs are bad, and the bad ones cost more than they earn.

**"Curtiu? Segue!" não funciona.** It asks for something and gives nothing. The audience has learned to ignore it, and it burns the last seconds of the video — the seconds that decide whether it loops.

What works better, in rough order:

1. **Nenhum.** A short loop is better without one. The follow comes from the content being good.
2. **Uma consequência, não um pedido.** "Se você não arrumar isso, vai acontecer de novo semana que vem" leaves the viewer with a reason to act, which is stronger than an instruction.
3. **Uma pergunta específica** that people can actually answer. "Qual foi o pior deploy que você já viu?" gets comments; "o que vocês acham?" gets nothing.
4. **Um próximo passo concreto**, when there really is one. "Tá no link" — once, not three times.

Put the CTA where it does not eat the loop. Frequently that means the caption or a pinned comment rather than the last four seconds of the video.

## A legenda

**A primeira linha é o produto.** It is the only part most people see before "mais". Do not waste it restating the video's title.

The caption has three possible jobs — pick one: extend the hook, add the context the video omitted, or ask the question that starts the comments. A caption doing all three does none.

Line breaks are deliberate. Short lines, air between them. A wall of text does not get read.

Hashtags: a few specific ones, at the end. They are a weak signal now and are not worth designing around.

## Adaptar de uma fonte

When the input is an article, a thread, a transcript or a teardown, the failure mode is compression — trying to fit everything in and ending with a list nobody remembers.

**Pegue uma ideia.** The best one. The rest is other videos, and saying so out loud is more useful to the user than cramming.

**Ache o específico.** Long-form sources hide their best moment in the middle — a number, an example, a moment. That is usually the hook, and it is rarely the source's own headline.

**Rewrite completely.** Written material read aloud sounds written. Take the idea, throw away the sentences.

When the source is a `viral-brand-analyst` teardown, the mechanism and the beat structure are already identified — use them rather than re-deriving. The teardown did that work, and its concepts are written at script resolution on purpose.
