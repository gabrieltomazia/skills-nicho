# Formatos

Structures that work, and when each earns its place. Pick by what the idea *is*, then time it — the shape should come from the content, not from a house template.

Timing note used throughout: PT-BR narration runs about **2,5–3 palavras por segundo**. Count words against seconds for every beat; a beat whose word count does not fit its duration will be rebuilt in the edit.

## 1. Reel / TikTok / Short (9:16)

The default output. Three length bands, and the choice matters more than anything inside it.

**7–15s — the loop.** One idea, one payoff, ends where it started so it replays seamlessly. Best for a single sharp observation or a visible result. No CTA; there is no room and it would eat the loop.

```
[0:00–0:02] gancho (resultado ou afirmação)
[0:02–0:10] a prova — uma coisa só
[0:10–0:13] o fecho, que devolve ao frame inicial
```

**20–40s — the working range.** Room for a real point with support. Most scripts land here.

```
[0:00–0:03] gancho
[0:03–0:08] por que isso importa / o custo do problema
[0:08–0:25] o conteúdo — 2 ou 3 batidas, uma ideia cada
[0:25–0:32] o fecho: consequência, não pedido
```

**45–90s — só com substância real.** A method, a story with a turn, a walkthrough. Needs a re-hook around 0:20 and another near 0:45 — a moment that restates the stakes or opens a new question, or retention drains.

**The failure to avoid** is the middle-length reel with thin content: long enough to demand commitment, too thin to reward it. When in doubt, cut to the shorter band; a tight 15s beats a slack 35s every time.

**Faceless specifics.** The voiceover is recorded after the fact, so the script has to work as a reading document: mark breath points, keep sentences short enough to say in one breath, and never write a line whose meaning depends on facial expression. The visual carries the hook — specify the first frame as carefully as the first line.

## 2. Carousel

A different product. People read carousels like a deck, and the metric that matters is **saves**, which means the piece has to be worth keeping.

- **8–10 slides.** Fewer feels thin, more loses people.
- **Slide 1 is the hook** and must be visibly incomplete — the 1→2 drop is the largest in the sequence by a wide margin.
- **One idea per slide.** If a slide needs two sentences to make sense, it is two slides.
- **Each slide ends by opening the next.** That is the entire mechanic of a carousel.
- **Last slide** earns the swipe: a summary worth saving, or a genuine CTA. Not "curtiu? segue!"

Write carousels as slide text plus a note on what each slide shows. Body copy is short — big type is the format's whole visual argument. Deliver it ready to lay out in Canva, Figma or any carousel tool.

## 3. VSL and long promo

2–10 minutes, selling. Structure is load-bearing here in a way it is not elsewhere.

```
Gancho — o problema, na linguagem de quem sofre com ele
Agitação — o custo de não resolver, concreto e específico
Prova — que você resolveu isso, para gente real
Mecanismo — por que a sua forma funciona (isso é o que diferencia)
Oferta — o que é, o que custa, o que acontece agora
Objeções — as três que o comprador realmente tem
CTA — um só, repetido
```

The mechanism section is where most VSLs collapse. "Nossa plataforma é intuitiva" is not a mechanism; *why* the approach works, specifically, is.

## 4. Talking-head recut

The user already recorded; the script's job is the *edit*, not the words. Deliver: which segments to keep with timestamps, where to cut dead air, where b-roll or inserts go, which sentences become on-screen text, and where the numbers or stat cards land.

Do not rewrite what they said. Cut it.

## 5. Long-form YouTube

10–30 minutes. Two things decide the outcome and neither is inside the video: **thumbnail and title**. Write those first, and treat the thumbnail as a hook with the same rigor as a spoken opener.

Then: cold open showing the outcome, chapter structure, and a re-hook every few minutes. Script the chapter cards and the insert beats rather than every sentence — long-form is too long to script word for word, and reading a full script aloud sounds worse than talking.

## Choosing when the user did not say

- They have something to **show** → Reel, result-before-method.
- They have something to **explain** with steps → carousel (saveable) or a 45–90s reel.
- They have a **story** → reel, 30–60s, turn in the middle.
- They are **selling** → VSL, or a short reel driving to one.
- They have **footage already** → recut.
- They have a **big topic** → long-form, with reels cut from it afterward.

When the request is genuinely open, the 20–40s reel is the right default: it is the cheapest to make, the fastest to test, and the easiest to cut down or expand once it works.
